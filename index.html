Formula and definitions

Transmission line. A path that carries waves between a source and a load. Z₀ is the ratio of voltage to current for a wave traveling in one direction.

Reflection coefficient Γ. The reflected voltage divided by the incident voltage at a particular point. Its magnitude measures the size of the reflection.

VSWR. The largest standing-wave voltage divided by the smallest. A value of 1 means the envelope is flat.

Admittance. The inverse of impedance. Its real and imaginary parts are conductance g and susceptance b after normalization.

Shunt stub. A branch of transmission line connected in parallel. A lossless shorted or open stub adds only susceptance, so the connection must be made where g already equals 1.

Smith chart in plain language

How to read the movement

1. The circle. The load starts on a constant-VSWR circle. Moving toward the generator rotates around that circle; the amount of reflection stays the same.

2. The centre. The centre is a perfect match: no reflected wave, normalized impedance z = 1 + j0.

3. The crossing. A shunt stub works where the conductance is already g = 1. The stub then cancels the remaining susceptance and moves the point to the centre.
